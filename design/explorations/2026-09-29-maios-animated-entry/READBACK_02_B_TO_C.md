# Readback 02 — Prototype B start/end

Date: 2026-09-29

## What the two screenshots establish

The operator supplied the initial and final visual states of Prototype B.

### Capture artifact to keep distinct from the page

The large cyan interval visible in the full-page capture is the scroll runway of
the sticky scene. During ordinary viewport scrolling the scene remains pinned
and occupies that interval; a full-page screenshot records the sticky surface
at one position and leaves the remaining document-height runway visible.

This is a capture/readback distinction, not proof of a literal blank panel in
normal interaction. It is still a design cost: the movement consumes nearly one
extra viewport of scroll.

### Material design result

Start and end are recognisably related, but too similar.

The end state adds labels, shrinks/moves the warm circle and reveals the three
routes, while the underlying mountain grammar remains essentially unchanged.
The visitor therefore sees an annotated landscape rather than a field that has
changed function.

Observed consequences:

- the final semantic labels are too small to become primary relations;
- the warm circle becomes a labelled MAIOS badge, but not yet a structural
  centre;
- the route strip is readable only as a late overlay;
- title opacity reduction weakens the first statement without giving enough
  space to a stronger second state;
- the next cream section repeats the route choice the end state has just
  introduced;
- full-page capture makes the long sticky runway visually expensive.

## Resultant for Prototype C

Do not increase animation quantity.

Change the carrier relation:

```text
mountain geometry
-> morphs into structured field geometry
-> semantic strata become legible
-> warm attractor becomes a system focus
-> real entry ports become primary actions
```

Use an explicit reversible exploration event for this test rather than a long
scroll runway. This makes start/end comparison direct and tests transformation
rather than scroll tolerance.

Prototype C is therefore a different experiment, not a polish pass on B.
